NEPS Project Team Equips Enumerators for Field Data Collection

November 2026

The Navigating Educational Pressures and Stressors (NEPS) Project has strengthened the capacity of eight research assistants to undertake field data collection through a one-day training held at the University of Skills Training and Entrepreneurial Development (USTED).

This is a follow-up session to a previous four-day training on standardised mental health research interviewing and assessment administration. Today’s training focused on enhancing participants’ technical and practical capacity to conduct high-quality field data collection through standardised, neutral and participant-sensitive questionnaire administration.

Participants were trained in the use of tablets and REDCap for secure data capture and submission, while adhering to approved study protocols and ethical principles, including informed consent, assent, confidentiality, safeguarding and privacy protection.

The training further addressed the identification of sensitive issues, symptom concerns and referral triggers within the approved study protocol, preparing the research assistants to respond appropriately to issues that may arise during field engagements.

Facilitated by Prof. Daniel Ansong, Mr. Bernard Arhin and Mr. Robert Annorhene, the training combined hands-on sessions, demonstrations, role-play, structured practice, case-based discussions, supervision and feedback.

The programme was delivered in four sessions. Mr. Bernard Arhin facilitated a hands-on REDCap session covering tablet use, navigation, data entry, submission and troubleshooting, while Prof. Daniel Ansong led discussions on key aspects of safeguarding procedures, legal frameworks, vulnerable groups and participant protection.

The Principal Investigator, Prof. Obed Brew, also shared insights from his experiences with safeguarding and offered practical advice on protecting participants and responding appropriately to sensitive situations during fieldwork.

Mr. Robert Annorhene assessed participants through question-and-answer sessions and final field-readiness assessment. The training was designed to ensure that the eight research assistants were practically trained, assessed and prepared for supervised field data collection.

Expected outputs from the exercise included assessment records, a pilot report documenting observations, challenges and corrective actions, as well as a final readiness list identifying enumerators approved for deployment or requiring additional support.
