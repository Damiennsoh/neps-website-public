Policy & advocacy

Turning research into decisions that matter.

NEPS connects evidence with youth perspectives, stakeholder dialogue and practical implementation questions. Policy translation is a process of learning and co-creation. [1]

Evidence-to-action pathway

From lived experience to practical decisions

  1. 01

    Listen and engage

    Bring youth narratives, lived experience and stakeholder priorities into the conversation.

  2. 02

    Synthesise evidence

    Combine research findings, contextual knowledge and implementation learning.

  3. 03

    Co-create options

    Develop policy briefs, practical recommendations and context-sensitive proposals together.

  4. 04

    Plan implementation

    Examine responsibilities, safeguarding, inclusion, referral, data governance and financing.

  5. 05

    Learn and adapt

    Review uptake and implementation experience before recommending wider application.

01

School wellbeing and safe environments

Policy work can connect mental-health literacy with school wellbeing standards, inclusion, safeguarding and safe-school approaches. Recommendations should reflect country systems and the evidence available.

02

Referral and connected services

The programme’s policy questions include how schools, communities and health services can work together through clear, locally appropriate referral pathways and defined responsibilities.

03

Data governance, implementation and financing

Evidence uptake requires attention to responsible data use, operational ownership, capacity, implementation costs and sustainable financing. Proposed pathways must be assessed with relevant stakeholders.
