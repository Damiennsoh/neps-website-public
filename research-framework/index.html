Research framework

Understanding the connections that shape wellbeing.

Young people’s lives are interconnected. NEPS studies how pressures, relationships and environments interact over time, using a relational wellbeing framework. [1]

01

Intersecting pressures

Educational pressure, poverty-related strain and mental-health-related stigma sit at the centre of the NEPS research questions. The programme investigates their interaction rather than assuming a single cause of distress. [1]

02

Context and protective support

Families, peers, schools and communities shape everyday experience. NEPS examines resilience, social support and help-seeking alongside pressures, recognising that experiences and opportunities differ between settings. [1]

03

Complementary evidence

Psychosocial assessments, qualitative and participatory work, biological, epigenetic and neurocognitive studies, and longitudinal digital monitoring contribute complementary perspectives. Specific procedures remain governed by approved study protocols. [1]

04

Learning over time

Repeated observations support questions about changing experiences and mental-health outcomes. A conceptual pathway does not itself establish causality, and intervention aims do not establish effectiveness.
