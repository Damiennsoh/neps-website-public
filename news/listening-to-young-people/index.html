Fieldwork preparation

NEPS project team equips enumerators for field data collection

A one-day practical session prepared eight research assistants for supervised field data collection, building on earlier training in research interviewing and assessment.

Fieldwork preparation

Publication date not specified in source

The Navigating Educational Pressures and Stressors (NEPS) Project has strengthened the capacity of eight research assistants to undertake field data collection through a one-day training held at the University of Skills Training and Entrepreneurial Development (USTED).

The session followed a previous four-day training on standardised mental health research interviewing and assessment administration. It focused on high-quality, standardised and participant-sensitive questionnaire administration.

Participants practised using tablets and REDCap for secure data capture and submission, with emphasis on approved study protocols, informed consent and assent, confidentiality, safeguarding and privacy protection. The training also covered how to recognise sensitive issues, symptom concerns and referral triggers and respond in line with the study protocol.

Facilitated by Prof. Daniel Ansong, Mr. Bernard Arhin and Mr. Robert Annorhene, the programme combined demonstrations, role-play, structured practice, case discussions, supervision and feedback. Mr. Arhin led the practical REDCap session, while Prof. Ansong discussed safeguarding procedures, legal frameworks, vulnerable groups and participant protection.

NEPS Principal Investigator Prof. Obed Brew shared practical safeguarding experience. Mr. Annorhene assessed participants through question-and-answer sessions and a final field-readiness assessment. The training was intended to prepare the research assistants for supervised fieldwork; the source document notes that assessment records, a pilot report and a readiness list were expected outputs.
